# The Weight of Seven Seats

*Empires of Dust: Taluma · Year 16 (3985 BCE) · Early Autumn, Day 25 · 83 days advanced*

The first succession charter, a cautious new horizon, and a knot that might one day carry Taluma's reputation.

## The Hall of Many Hands

The Hall of Many Hands had hosted arguments over fish, timber, infants and trade. This time Esha asked the households to consider what would happen when the seven people sitting before them could no longer serve.

Dava had prepared a careful proposal: vacancies would be announced publicly, households could nominate willing adults, and the remaining council would appoint a temporary caretaker until the community chose a successor. The caretaker could keep water, food and repairs running but could not bind Taluma to war or a treaty. Each major council decision would carry the names of those who supported it and those who opposed it. No seat would pass from parent to child.

"Who gets to come here and nominate someone?" Seli asked. "A fisher who is out at the tide line? A mother keeping a sick child warm?"

Iset pointed out that a hearing attended only by those with spare hours would soon become rule by those with spare hours. Nemi feared the opposite danger: leaving a seat vacant too long while disputes multiplied. Maro marked the disagreement instead of erasing it.

The resulting Seven Seats charter was a complete draft, not law. Esha asked for another round of household testimony before the guiding intelligence decided whether to adopt it. The Listening Bench gained a real responsibility, but no votes or council seats.

## Two tides at Windcut

Far south, Tidefin reached Windcut with Rela at the paddle and the revised bindings wrapped against salt. Vara met them outside the fishers' working stakes. Vessa and Maren had sent two comparably sized lashings and replaceable reed sleeves, each marked so a failed knot could be distinguished from a worn fiber. No one was asked to risk a loaded fish barrier.

When a rough tide passed, the ordinary cord had crept loose in the grit. The newly sleeved braid remained tied, but its outer fibers had begun to wear. The difference mattered, yet no one called the new design storm-proof.

Vara offered to compare further samples at two later seasonal visits, provided the fishers agreed again each time. Taluma returned with evidence, not a contract.

## Beyond the old boundaries

Letha took Reedwing north with two other Taluman crew and Keshu, who volunteered as a Crescent pilot. The Ternhook households accepted one sheltered return stop. Beyond the already observed mouth of Northreach Estuary, Letha glimpsed a low dark ridge unfamiliar to her old chart. Farther in, something pale rose above the shore. Smoke, perhaps; spray or mist, just as plausibly.

Keshu pointed to a shoal cut where the tide was turning. Letha ordered the boat about. The ridge entered the atlas with a witness mark; the supposed smoke received a question mark.

Inland, Ilan returned to Upper Path to seek permission, not passage by assumption. Oru and Tesh consulted the seasonal Ridge Hearth households, who accepted a single escorted visit to an upper shoulder beyond the old Windgrass stopping place. There the path keeper Renka showed the visitors a loose-stone descent. They did not follow it. Instead she examined their carrying-frame lashings and suggested a future craft meeting at an agreed site.

There was no new road, quarry or treaty. There was, however, someone beyond the old frontier who might now recognize them when they returned.
