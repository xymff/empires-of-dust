# The Seats and the Stones

*Empires of Dust: Taluma · Year 16 (3985 BCE) · Early Winter, Day 96 · 71 days advanced*

Taluma makes its founding principles binding and learns why an unmarked path can be an act of friendship.

## A charter that outlives its authors

For the first time in Taluma's history, the Council of the Shore opened a hearing to decide rules that would outlive the people making them. The Hall of Many Hands filled twice, not once. At dawn, fishers returning from the tide flats spoke before sleeping. At dusk, people responsible for children and elders could attend while other adults covered their work.

Dava stood beside Maro's clay witness board and read the three principles the guiding intelligence had authorized. Any resident household could openly nominate a willing successor. No council seat could be inherited. And consequential decisions, including dissent, had to be recorded where people could hear them read back and challenge an inaccurate account.

Nemi placed his hand on the unfinished section of the board. "And when someone dies in the hungry part of winter, how long must we argue before the work is done?"

"We are deciding what the seat belongs to," Dava replied, "not who gets the next one."

The principles were ratified. Caretaker duration, the final selection threshold and appeals remained open for a separately witnessed charter amendment. Seven councillors remained in office. No apprentice gained a vote.

## Renka's upper shelf

Inland, Ilan's party spent days arranging a meeting rather than cutting straight toward Windgrass Hollow. Oru and Tesh obtained consent from Ridge Hearth households for an escorted visit to a sheltered shelf chosen by Renka. Ilan brought Kelan and Leni, a sample of Taluman carrying-frame cordage, and no tools for quarrying or clearing a path.

Renka showed how a pebble marker could guide a welcome guest and also invite a stranger into another family's resting ground. Kelan demonstrated a replaceable fiber sleeve that protected a hauling knot from grit. A Ridge Hearth craftsperson answered by shifting the load point on a wooden frame.

A short downhill trial carried the weight more steadily. On damp grass, however, the revised hitch slipped and the load had to be caught before it struck a stone. They recorded both results. Nobody declared a new transport invention.

## The sign that was not placed

A moon later, Renka and a willing companion came to Taluma, accompanied along accepted approaches by Oru and Tesh. They chose a public gathering at the Hall and could leave whenever they wished.

Vessa and Maren showed how sacrificial reed sleeves could be replaced without discarding an entire cord. Renka repeated the frame adjustment but declined to demonstrate certain trail signs away from her community.

When an apprentice proposed permanent stone markers between the communities, Renka overturned the proposed marker with her foot. Ilan suggested a removable sign placed only during a jointly agreed meeting and taken away afterward. Renka accepted it as a proposal, not continuing access.

Taluma learned that a stronger friendship sometimes required leaving a trail unmarked.
