# The House With Empty Places

*Empires of Dust: Taluma · Year 17 (3984 BCE) · Early Winter, Day 132 · 64 days advanced*

Taluma tests a seasonal food market, spends its long-protected house kit, and learns that lasting friendship begins with the right to decline.

## I. The baskets at the door

The first visitor to question Taluma’s new seasonal food market was not a stranger. It was Iset, watching Aret place three inspected fish packets beneath the Hall’s eastern awning. “If one packet fails,” she asked, “whose mark is the buyer meant to trust?”

Aret did not answer with another mark. She invited the Shellbend and Reedmouth guests to choose their own sample from each offered lot. When one selected packet proved damp at its center, she removed the whole suspect batch from sale. Dava recorded the rejection beside the names of the people who had checked it. The visiting fisher watched the correction, then agreed to inspect a different lot.

Nemi had wanted a market that might return at the change of each season. What he obtained was two successful, separately invited gatherings, with no guaranteed third. A Shellbend household took cured fish; Reedmouth traders brought different edible stores and practical wrapping materials. Fourteen settlement-days of inspected Taluman food went outward and five days of other provisions came in. A few poles and reeds received in barter were entered under materials, not food. The rejected packets counted against Taluma’s own spoilage.

An apprentice asked whether the traders could simply take the same amount next season. “Not until they agree to come,” Maro said. “And not until we know the food is fit to leave.” Aret’s new comparisons were encouraging: eleven of twelve carefully raised-and-screened trial lots remained sound at inspection, against nine of twelve older covered lots. No one called that a guarantee through a long voyage, and the public tally said so.

## II. The roof before the family

On a dry morning, Seli led several households to a small prepared plot within Taluma’s existing settlement. The village had only four unoccupied housing places. The five-person home kit had been kept apart in the material ledger for years: four poles, seven reed bundles, one stone load and one cordage coil. Today, for the first time, its witnesses authorized those materials to be used rather than merely guarded.

Hadan and the repair crew raised the framework while volunteers prepared reed panels and a sheltered cooking approach. Nobody was ordered away from caregiving or from essential water and food work. When a gust lifted one roof edge, Seli insisted on another fastening before anyone moved inside. The correction delayed completion by several days and used part of the ordinary repair allotment.

Dava counted the house twice: once as a structure and once as a responsibility. There would be ongoing roof inspections, fuel needs and space for people who had not yet arrived. Esha agreed that an empty house was not evidence of a promised migration. Taluma had built room for five more residents, but had not compelled a marriage, invited a large group, or claimed any new shore.

At the second inspection, rain ran away from the threshold. The new home passed its practical checks, bringing capacity from 113 to 118 places. All 109 residents remained in their existing households. Nine places stood open, including the newly prepared home. The kit’s materials were now spent and recorded as construction, not still described as an untouched reserve.

## III. A meeting that can be declined

Ilan carried Taluma’s proposed seasonal gathering marks only as far as Upper Path. Oru and Tesh took the question to Renka’s household; no one followed them uninvited. When Renka chose to meet on the familiar Upper Windgrass shelf, Kelan brought a familiar frame and no new load to test on the dangerous descent.

Renka was interested in meeting again. She was less interested in strangers assuming the same route would always be theirs. She drew a short line in the damp ground, placed two pebbles for two possible seasons, and wiped away the line between them. Leni understood enough to ask for the terms to be repeated by demonstration.

The households present agreed to a modest arrangement: during each of the next two seasonal windows, Taluma might request one escorted craft meeting at a location chosen by the Ridge Hearth hosts. A separate meeting at Taluma’s Hall could be offered in return. Each actual visit required fresh assent from the relevant people, and either side could decline or cancel without penalty. No permanent path signs, unescorted access, exclusive trade or rights over another household’s land followed from the agreement.

Kelan showed the frame’s low-load support, now credited in the teaching record to him, Renka and her companion. A strap still pulled sideways when turned sharply, so Renka kept the demonstration on safe ground. The day ended not with a new road, but with a clear way to ask for the next meeting.

When the report reached the Hall, Iset asked whether the seasonal pebbles meant Taluma could go inland twice without waiting for a reply. Maro answered in the public record: “They mean we know how to ask. Nothing more.” Seven councillors remained at their seats, and the story of the agreement was read aloud with both its possibilities and its limits.
