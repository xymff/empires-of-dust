# The Price of Plenty

*Empires of Dust: Taluma · Year 16 (3985 BCE) · Late Spring, Day 295 · 64 days advanced*

Taluma's storage apprentices learn to distrust their own marks, Renka refuses unsafe shortcuts, and a child is born.

## A different kind of harvest

At Taluma's oldest drying house, Aret cut the tie on a basket marked three times and counted twice. The fish near the rim was sound; the center had become damp.

"If the mark only means somebody looked at it," Dava said, "we haven't learned anything."

Aret and six rotating apprentices separated small lots between tightly covered shelves and raised cross-ventilated racks. Each received preparation and inspection marks. The ventilated lots stayed drier, but an open screen admitted insects until Seli insisted on a narrower weave. Four settlement-days of questionable stores were rejected in the turn.

Fourteen settlement-days of inspected food left Taluma through modest exchanges and gifts. Five days came back as other preserved provisions, alongside distinct craft materials. When a trader suggested a much larger shipment, Nemi refused to treat tomorrow's reserves as today's bargaining chips.

"Next year's hungry people do not get a vote in today's bargain," he said.

## Renka's lesson

At Upper Windgrass, Ilan and Kelan met Renka through Oru and Tesh, after the host households invited them. The dangerous descent remained closed.

Kelan widened the shoulder support on his frame and revised its gripping sleeve. Ten of twelve wet-ground trials succeeded on level terrain. Two loads shifted on sharp turns. Renka stopped the experiment before anyone tried the treacherous slope.

The visitors shared inspected food and packing methods while Renka's people demonstrated a different weight distribution. A future invitation was offered but no permanent passage was granted.

## The public record and a new life

Iset challenged a proposed food shipment for being discussed while many fishers were at sea. The council held a second hearing and reduced the shipment to the fourteen settlement-days in the accounts.

Reedwing also completed a provisioned northern reconnaissance using an approved stopping place. Beyond Northreach, the crew saw broken rock and difficult shoals. They turned back instead of attempting an uncertain passage.

On Day 278, Kiva gave birth to Nila. Ralen cared for the household while their older child Aven waited with a folded reed mat. The birth brought Taluma to 109 residents, with no deaths or migration.

Esha listened as the next council reading announced the number. One hundred and nine.
