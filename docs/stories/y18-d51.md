# The Space Between Hearths

*Empires of Dust: Taluma · Year 18 (3983 BCE) · Mid Autumn, Day 51 · 70 days advanced*

A grown founding child chooses an independent hearth, Taluma audits seasonal trade, and the northern rocks remain beyond safe knowledge.

## I. A hearth of one's own

The first request came from Nes, a young adult who had been a child when Taluma's first families gathered beside the lagoon. Nes had grown into a careful maker of basket frames but still shared a family hearth. Now the work crowded the household's sleeping space, and Nes asked Seli whether a person could live separately without first finding a spouse.

Seli brought the question to the Hall of Many Hands instead of treating it as a marriage problem. Maro's voluntary census had found nineteen unpartnered adults, and nine founding-era children who were now grown but still shared their original family hearths. Neither number revealed what anyone wanted. Some preferred their extended households. Others supported relatives who needed care. None owed Taluma a wedding or a move.

Nes and a relative inspected an unused room already counted among Taluma's 118 housing places. The repairs used ordinary unearmarked reeds and lashings, not the construction kit spent on the five-place home. The council recorded a separate cooking hearth only after Nes requested it and the space passed Seli's inspection. Nes still visited the family table. Another grown adult asked for information about leaving and chose to stay.

At the public readback, Seli reported twenty-nine hearth households, one more than before. Eight of the original nine grown founding children still shared family homes. The nineteen unpartnered adults remained nineteen, including sixteen working-age adults and three elders. Population was still 112, with six unoccupied housing places. 'You haven't left us,' Nes's relative said. 'No,' Nes answered. 'I just have a door I can close.'

## II. What survives the crossing

Under Taluma's market awning, Aret made an apprentice set the travel costs beside the food tally. Dava prepared one card for what Taluma sent, another for what came back, and a third for the crew time, fuel and repairs spent moving it. Iset had demanded the third card after hearing too many exchanges described as if the boats rowed themselves.

A Shellbend household and a Reedmouth household each accepted another invitation. A Crescent Shoal craft visitor independently offered prepared poles and reeds. The buyer-selected food samples were opened in front of both parties. One lot failed its center-dryness check and was withdrawn, with the loss entered beside ordinary spoilage rather than hidden inside the sales numbers.

Across two separately consented exchanges, twelve settlement-days of Taluman food went out and five days of food came back. Two wood poles and three reed bundles entered the materials register. Three food-days were rejected or spoiled across the turn. On another modest comparison, fourteen of sixteen raised-screen lots remained sound at inspection, compared with twelve of sixteen on the older covered shelves. Aret refused to convert those small tests into promises of storm-proof storage.

The extra goods were useful. So were a visitor's better packing method and the fact that returning buyers felt free to reject a doubtful packet. But Nemi would not call a net loss of seven food-days a food profit. Smoke fuel, coastal crew labor, household hospitality and basket repairs had all cost something. When a recorder suggested writing 'reliable network' next to the three shore names, Maro shook his head. Each household could decline its next invitation. Taluma was gaining trust, not signing away its stores. The provisional 180-day food floor remained intact.

## III. A line that stays outside the rocks

Ilan waited at Upper Path while Oru and Tesh carried a fresh question to Renka. The previous approved meeting created no continuing right to use her people's tracks. Renka chose one sheltered bench on gentle Upper Windgrass ground and agreed to meet there. Kelan brought a carrying frame and spare cord, not quarrying tools or gear for the forbidden loose-stone descent.

A wet quick-release strap shifted in one early turn, tipping an empty practice basket into the grass. Renka stopped the work. Her companion demonstrated how a tired carrier could lower the load before releasing the shoulder band, instead of trusting a knot to behave under strain. Ten of twelve later controlled carries stayed secure; two shifted during turns. Leni credited Kelan and both Ridge Hearth teachers. There was no steep-ground qualification, no copying of private trail signs, and no standing permission to return.

Back at the Hall, Letha spread the existing Northreach hide atlas before Keshu. Six prior soundings marked known safe offshore water through two tidal states. They did not tell anyone what lay behind the narrow rock notch. Keshu traced the line where a cross-current had persisted even when the surface looked deceptively calm. Letha proposed repeating flood and ebb readings from a carefully chosen offshore observation station, never inside the notch, with explicit retreat limits and a fresh Ternhook stopping invitation.

They tested the proposed sounding marks in Taluma's sheltered inlet first. A wet rope made some marks hard to distinguish. The learners widened their spacing and checked them again. Neither Reedwing nor Tidefin sailed toward Northreach this turn. The boats remained available for ordinary permitted travel. A blank remained beyond the rocks on the atlas, where a blank belonged.

At the season's final gathering, Nes sat near relatives from the old hearth. Taluma had made room for an independent life without requiring a marriage, learned to count the costs of trade without losing its appetite for exchange, and refused to mistake a safer plan for a safe passage. Esha listened to the report and asked the apprentice recorder to read every limitation aloud.
